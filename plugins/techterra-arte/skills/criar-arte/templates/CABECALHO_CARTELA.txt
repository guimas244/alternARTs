Use case: game creature art sheet for Techterra.
The attached scorpion image is a RENDERING REFERENCE ONLY. Copy ONLY its technique: clean variable-width
outline in a very dark shade of each creature's own color (never pure black); cel-shading with flat color,
one soft shadow band and sharp specular highlights on hard surfaces; key light from top-front plus a thin
rim light on the upper edges; TWO dominant colors per creature (a dark body color and a light accent);
fine speckle texture ONLY on the light accent, all other surfaces smooth.
Do NOT copy anything else from the scorpion: not its anatomy, not its face, not its eyes, not its angry
brow, not its brown-and-sand palette, not its crouching pose.
The second attached image (a board of 12 approved creatures from this game) is a VARIETY REFERENCE ONLY:
it shows how different each creature's anatomy, head, eyes and silhouette must be from the others
(serpent, living armor, living frame, pipe organ, cauldron, robot, goat, tree giant…). Aim for that level
of difference between the new creatures. Do NOT copy any of those 12 designs.

FACES AND EYES MUST BE DIFFERENT FOR EVERY CREATURE. Each creature's head, face and eyes are designed from
its OWN concept and follow its own description: eye shape (round, slit, compound, button, crescent, star
pupil, hollow sockets…), number (none, one, two, three, many), size, placement (on the head, on petals,
inside a flower, on stalks, on the chest) and color. Some creatures may have no face at all and glow from
inside instead. Never reuse the same head or face template on different bodies; never give everyone the
same pair of narrow glowing angry eyes with a V-shaped brow. Every creature has some self-lit element
(eyes or another part named in its description), with a short halo, and nothing else glows.
Expression and pose follow each creature's personality in its description, within the POWER LADDER
below: low tiers can be curious, sleepy, shy or cheerful; high tiers are imposing, menacing, ancient or
majestic. Three-quarter front view, a lively pose, never a static catalog pose. Camera height follows
the POWER LADDER.

{GRADE}
FILL THE CELL: each creature must be drawn LARGE, its longest dimension spanning about 90% of the cell
(width or height, whichever is bigger), leaving only a thin empty transparent margin of about 5% on each
side. This is an approximate artistic guideline, not an exact measurement. Do not draw small creatures
floating in empty space. Keep each creature's NATURAL body shape: only its LONGEST side reaches about 90%
of the cell. A standing humanoid or tall plant is tall and narrow (fills the height, not the width); a
serpent or long beast is wide and low (fills the width, not the height); only round or crouching bodies
fill both. NEVER squash, crouch, curl or fold a creature just to make it fill the square, and do not make
every creature a round compact blob. Only fold a tail, wing, vine or limb inward when it would otherwise
leave the cell. The whole
creature stays inside its cell; never touch, cross or overlap neighboring cells.
All creatures share the same line weight, finish and scale of detail, as one consistent set.
No floor, no ground, no cast shadow, no scenery, no background texture, no grid lines, no loose particles
or glow outside the creature's body.
ABSOLUTELY NO TEXT: no letters, numbers, labels, runes, pseudo-writing, logos, signatures or watermarks.
One creature per cell, no second creature, no rider, no frame, no interface element.

DUAL TYPING RULE: every creature has TWO types and is a true fusion of both. The primary type ({TIPO})
defines the concept and anatomy. The secondary type changes the MATERIAL, COLOR and behavior of that
creature. A creature does NOT need the stereotypical color of its primary type: follow the colors given
in each description, which come from the combination of both types. The creatures must look clearly
different from each other in color, material and silhouette, never recolors of one design.
Cyan is reserved for Electric energy: only creatures with the Electric type may use it, and only on their
electric parts.

POWER LADDER — the tier must be readable at first glance, WITHOUT any text:
- Tier 1–2: a small, harmless critter or hatchling; soft rounded forms, big head, short limbs; camera
  slightly ABOVE looking down; cute, curious or shy.
- Tier 3–4: a young, agile creature; first defensive features (small thorns, a shell, claws); camera at
  eye level; alert and a bit cocky.
- Tier 5–6: an adult and clearly dangerous creature; real weapons (hooked thorns, jaws, spikes, horns),
  solid muscular or armored mass; camera at eye level; aggressive or cunning.
- Tier 7–8: an apex predator or ancient guardian; heavy massive body, armor plates, multiple weapons,
  a battle-worn detail (scar, broken spike); camera slightly BELOW looking up; imposing, threatening,
  commanding pose (rearing, arms spread, jaws open, looming).
- Tier 9–10: a titan, living legend, calamity or deity; colossal presence that dominates the cell,
  crown-like or halo-like structures, overwhelming weaponry, energy running through the whole body;
  camera LOW looking up (heroic worm's-eye perspective); majestic, terrifying or god-like. It must look
  like a final boss, never like a cute critter or a decorative plant.
Higher tiers also get denser detail, harder contrast and stronger self-light. Drawing size in the cell
stays similar; POWER comes from anatomy, weaponry, posture, expression and camera, not from scale.
